# Turning prevention into meaningful work

Use this route to connect long-term goals, family context and everyday hazards with prevention. Build on `HOS-FND-01`, `HOS-FND-02`, `HOS-FND-10` and `HOS-FND-12` as relevant.

Units: PA-01 turns values into capabilities; PA-02 improves a family-history question; PA-03 examines preventable injury. The exercises are learning activities until the user adopts a separate action.

## HOS-DEP-PA-01 — Work backward from the life you want to preserve

**Purpose:** Make long-horizon prevention specific enough to guide priorities.

A desired future life contains activities, relationships and responsibilities, not just favorable laboratory values. The supplied future-capability worksheet prompts reflection on independence, connection and meaningful activity. Miller and Sumser add that the person's own account of a worthwhile life may evolve with experience; a treatment or procedure checklist does not fully express those values.

Use a new exercise: imagine wanting to visit a friend independently, join a conversation, and return home without exhausting the rest of the day. Break that experience into requirements: navigating, getting there, moving through the environment, hearing and communicating, managing energy, and maintaining the relationship. This is an illustrative teaching example, not a diagnosis of missing abilities or a prescribed training target.

Next distinguish what matters from what is known. Which capacities are already adequate? Which have changed? What information would help decide whether the main constraint is physical, sensory, cognitive, emotional, practical or something else? The prevention plan can then focus on a relevant constraint rather than optimize the easiest available number.

A capability can motivate action while leaving room for adaptation. If circumstances change, revise the desired activity or support required without pretending the earlier goal never existed. In HealthOS, the result is a clear rationale for a possible priority; it becomes an intervention only through a separate decision.

**Source basis:** [W-FUTURE](SOURCES.md#w-future), physical pages 2–3; [D354](SOURCES.md#d354), E354-19/20, 01:27:03–01:28:02. The visit example and decomposition exercise are original teaching applications.

**Check:** A learner wants a better fitness percentile. How could a capability-based question make that goal more useful without dismissing the measurement?

**Expected answer:** Ask which valued activity the capacity should support, what limits that activity and how progress would be observed. The percentile can inform comparison, but does not replace the functional goal or establish every necessary capacity.

## HOS-DEP-PA-02 — Improve the family-history question before drawing a conclusion

**Purpose:** Turn a vague inherited-risk concern into a better evidence-gathering task.

“Heart trouble runs in the family” is a useful starting signal but an incomplete description. The supplied family-history worksheet asks for the relationship, age or birth year, age at onset, condition, treatment and, where relevant, cause of death. Those details distinguish a named event from an impression and make the time horizon visible. Its prompts span cardiovascular, neurological, cancer, metabolic and other conditions rather than limiting history to one familiar diagnosis.

Practice with a fictional account: a relative remembers an uncle having “a heart operation,” but neither the procedure nor age is known. A useful next conversation asks what happened, when, who knows directly and whether a record exists. It does not silently relabel the event as a heart attack or inherit a genetic diagnosis. A blank answer is an unknown, not evidence that the condition was absent.

HealthOS adds the evidence-handling layer: retain who supplied the account, its date and confidence, and distinguish reported family history from confirmed records or genetic results. Keep the initial statement when a later detail corrects it. The value is a more specific question for prevention and care, not a risk score invented from a family tree.

For a selected learning exercise, identify the one missing detail most likely to change interpretation. Gathering every possible family detail is less useful than resolving an uncertainty that matters to an actual decision.

**Source basis:** [W-FAMILY](SOURCES.md#w-family), physical page 2. Provenance, unknown-state and correction handling apply the existing [HealthOS ledger rules](../LEDGER_AND_INGESTION.md).

**Check:** Does “no family history documented” mean a negative family history? What should be retained when a second relative corrects the first account?

**Expected answer:** No; documentation is absent. Retain both dated accounts and their sources, record the uncertainty or explicit correction, and make the current interpretation traceable.

## HOS-DEP-PA-03 — Prevention includes making ordinary errors less consequential

**Purpose:** Apply preventive reasoning to everyday injury exposure as well as disease markers.

Rosekind's road-safety discussion separates the event that initiates a crash from the conditions that make it severe. An investigation that stops at the last human error may miss road layout, speed, separation, protective systems or other contributing factors. Preventing a mistake and reducing its consequences are related but distinct opportunities.

Attention is also more than eye and hand position. The source distinguishes mental distraction from simply holding a phone and describes impairment before someone falls asleep. Feeling alert or familiar with an activity is not proof of unchanged performance. These points support a preventive habit of checking the conditions before exposure, rather than relying only on confidence once the task begins.

Use a fictional near-miss as an exercise. Identify the initiating event, upstream conditions, what could have prevented the conflict and what could have reduced injury if it occurred. Separate observed facts from an attractive story about blame. Do not add overlapping risk-factor percentages as if each described a separate set of crashes.

The source's vehicle technology and policy descriptions belong to its recording date and jurisdiction. The durable HealthOS addition is broader: ordinary injury prevention can deserve attention alongside laboratory optimization. A source-specific driving rule or equipment decision needs current local context, while the reasoning exercise remains useful across settings.

**Source basis:** [D295](SOURCES.md#d295), E295-08/09, 00:45:30/00:53:15; E295-11/12, 00:54:44–00:55:44; E295-16/17, 01:16:07–01:19:12; E295-19/22, 01:28:08/01:32:48; caveat at 00:31:11.

**Check:** If a crash report identifies the driver's last error, why is the preventive review not finished?

**Expected answer:** The last event does not describe every upstream condition or injury-severity factor. Review prevention of the conflict, mitigation of consequences and evidence for each explanation separately.
