## HOS-DEP-NU-01 — Protein needs begin with the job to be done

**Purpose:** Explain why different protein numbers can answer different questions.

Maintaining nitrogen balance, supporting training, retaining muscle during weight loss, and preventing disability are different outcomes. A study designed around one cannot automatically establish the best intake for another. Resistance training provides a stimulus for adaptation; dietary protein supplies amino acids. More building material does not replace the stimulus.

The source discussion describes most of the additional training benefit near 1.6 grams per kilogram daily, with diminishing returns above that in the studies considered. A practical target around 2 grams was also proposed to allow for missed meals. The first is an interpretation of a response curve; the second is a clinician's buffer. Neither establishes a universal requirement. A plateau means smaller additional benefit on the measured outcome, which is a different claim from either toxicity or unlimited safety.

The calculation also needs a defined weight basis. An illustrative 80-kilogram body weight multiplied by 1.6 gives 128 grams daily; that arithmetic does not decide whether the target fits the person. Substantial excess body fat can make current-weight calculations misleading. Kidney disease, pregnancy, growth, and other clinical circumstances require their own guidance.

Finally, protein has to fit a complete diet. An intake that crowds out enough energy, carbohydrate for training, essential fats, fibre, or enjoyable meals can defeat the original goal. Assess function, adequacy, practicality, and the relevant health context together.

**Expected answer:** A plateau is not evidence of harm; harms need relevant outcomes and follow-up. Identify the person's goal, training, energy balance, weight basis, clinical circumstances, and what other foods the target would displace.

## HOS-DEP-NU-02 — A lighter body can be better fueled or under-fueled

**Purpose:** Distinguish useful body-composition change from inadequate support for daily life and training.

Scale weight combines tissues, water, stored carbohydrate, and digestive contents. A decline therefore does not reveal how much fat or muscle changed. Even a lean-tissue estimate includes components other than contractile muscle. Pair comparable measurements with strength, recovery, energy, symptoms, and the ability to perform ordinary tasks.

Under-fueling can develop without deliberate restriction. Early training, a busy day, exercise-related appetite suppression, gastrointestinal discomfort, or appetite-suppressing medication can leave too little opportunity to eat. A high protein total does not establish adequate total energy or carbohydrate. For someone struggling with meal volume, planned portable food and more energy-dense choices may solve a different problem from the lower-energy substitutions useful during intentional fat loss.

Menstrual changes can provide another clue, but waiting for periods to disappear misses earlier opportunities to notice fatigue, repeated injury, and poor recovery. Appearance is also unreliable: a person can look athletic while intake fails to support their workload. Midlife fatigue may overlap with menopausal symptoms, so the timeline should include both fueling and reproductive stage.

When weight loss is an appropriate goal, success should include what is preserved: strength, useful lean tissue, adequate nutrition, and a sustainable routine. A stable scale with improved function or body composition can represent progress. A rapid decline accompanied by worsening function deserves reassessment rather than automatic celebration.

**Check:** A fictional exerciser loses weight, meets a protein target, and becomes more tired with poorer workouts. Why is the protein total insufficient reassurance?

**Expected answer:** It does not establish enough total energy or carbohydrate, adequate recovery, or preservation of muscle and function. Review intake timing, appetite, workload, symptoms, and comparable measurements before interpreting the weight loss.

## HOS-DEP-NU-03 — Every dietary change has a replacement

**Purpose:** Identify the comparison hidden inside claims that a food is healthy or harmful.

Replacing one fat with another at the same energy intake asks a different question from adding oil to an unchanged diet. Removing a snack and replacing it with fruit differs again from buying the same snack made with a different oil. Describe what is removed, what replaces it, and what else changes before interpreting the outcome.

This matters in arguments about seed oils. The source discussion evaluates unsaturated fats as replacements for saturated fats, considering lipoprotein changes alongside other evidence. It does not establish that unlimited oil is beneficial. A biochemical property, such as susceptibility to oxidation, also cannot determine the whole effect on health by itself; exposure, preparation, other pathways, and clinical outcomes matter. Repeatedly heated frying oil is a distinct exposure from modest use of fresh oil, and the best frying-fat comparison remains unresolved in this source.

Processing labels create a similar problem. Pasteurization, fermentation, protein concentration, and manufacturing a highly palatable snack are different operations. A rule to reduce packaged snack foods may help someone improve their diet without proving that every processing step has the same effect.

If someone feels better after eliminating seed oils, ask whether they also changed restaurant meals, snacks, portions, fibre, or total energy. Their improvement is worth understanding. It does not isolate the ingredient as the cause. The useful next comparison follows the person's goal rather than defending a food category.

**Source basis:** [D380](SOURCES.md#d380), E380-01 at 00:04:05, E380-18 at 01:25:35, E380-24 at 01:51:59, E380-25 at 01:53:22, and E380-29 at 02:04:04; [D368](SOURCES.md#d368), E368-24 at 01:34:17 and E368-26 at 01:20:44.

**Check:** A person stops eating chips, takeaway meals, and commercial sauces to avoid one ingredient, then feels better. Name two explanations besides that ingredient and a comparison that would distinguish them.

**Expected answer:** Plausible changes include total energy, portions, overall food quality, fibre, or meal patterns. A useful comparison would hold the relevant surrounding pattern reasonably stable while changing the proposed cause, with a defined outcome; the original observation alone cannot isolate it.

## HOS-DEP-NU-04 — Ketones describe a fuel state, not a health verdict

**Purpose:** Separate achieving ketosis from demonstrating that a dietary strategy serves its intended purpose.

During fasting or carbohydrate restriction, the liver can increase ketone production, supplying an alternative fuel. A blood ketone concentration reflects both production and use. A lower reading during activity may therefore accompany greater utilization; chasing the highest possible number can misread the process.

A ketone supplement changes the measurement through another route. Supplying ketones does not reproduce all the effects of a dietary pattern, including its energy intake, food composition, and sustained changes in fuel availability. Products also differ: a mineral salt, an ester, and a precursor requiring liver metabolism are not equivalent interventions merely because each can raise a ketone result.

Evaluate the intended outcome separately. Therapeutic ketogenic diets for selected epilepsy populations have a different evidence base and clinical setting from proposed applications in dementia or cancer. In the source discussion, a proposed glucose-to-ketone target for cancer research was not a validated survival target. An attractive metabolic explanation cannot supply the missing clinical comparison.

Nutrition still has to support the whole person. The guest describes losing strength after sharply reducing protein and experiencing adverse LDL and ApoB changes despite ketosis. Those personal observations illustrate why ketones or glucose alone cannot establish success. A useful assessment includes the actual goal, adequate nourishment, function, cardiovascular markers when relevant, tolerability, and the burden of maintaining the pattern.

**Check:** A ketone drink raises a person's ketone measurement. What has that demonstrated, and what has it left unanswered?
