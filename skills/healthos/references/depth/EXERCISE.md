# Physical capacity and training decisions

**Purpose:** Connect meaningful physical goals with distinct capacities, interpretable measurements, and training decisions that fit the person and situation.

## HOS-DEP-EX-01 — Work backward from an activity

**Purpose:** Translate a real-life goal into several capacities without replacing it with one fitness score.

Start with an activity the person values and describe its demands. Carrying shopping home may involve walking for a sustained period, holding a load, climbing steps, and recovering balance on uneven ground. A goal becomes more informative when distance, load, terrain, and the need for pauses are clear. The purpose of specifying these details is to choose relevant evidence, not to impose another person's performance standard.

Muscle mass describes tissue quantity; strength describes force; power adds how quickly force is produced. Balance and coordination help direct that force. Aerobic capacity and local muscular endurance support repeated work, but one does not establish the other. A person can improve a gym lift while remaining uncomfortable carrying an awkward bag outdoors.

Separate exposure, capacity, and function. Recorded walking minutes describe exposure. A standardized strength assessment describes a particular capacity. Completing the shopping trip describes function, influenced by several capacities and the environment. Each can improve without an identical change in the others. Likewise, a muscle-mass estimate cannot certify strength or balance. Select a small set of observations that reveals the actual limiting demand, retaining the test conditions and the person's experience. Long-term continuity matters alongside the best result achieved once.

**Source basis:** [D349](SOURCES.md#d349), E349-01 [00:09:24] and E349-31 [01:39:06]; [D250](SOURCES.md#d250), E250-24 [02:24:29], E250-30 [02:32:24], and E250-31 [02:34:11].

**Check:** A learner walks more each week and has a higher muscle-mass estimate, but still struggles to carry shopping upstairs. Which improvement is documented, and which capacities remain uncertain?

**Expected answer:** Walking exposure increased and the body-composition method produced a higher estimate. Neither establishes better carrying strength, local endurance, balance, or stair-climbing function. Clarify the limiting part of the task and compare relevant observations under consistent conditions.

## HOS-DEP-EX-02 — Compare programs by their purpose and audience

**Purpose:** Understand training dose as a conditional choice rather than a universal number of sessions or sets.

A useful stimulus must be demanding enough to support the intended adaptation and recoverable enough to repeat. That balance changes with experience, current capacity, injury history, available time, and other demands. Beginners may first need practice and confidence; experienced trainees may need a more specific stimulus. Early success can include returning willingly for the next session, even before substantial changes in strength are measurable.

This explains several apparent disagreements. A brief twice-weekly whole-body structure for general health, a longer beginner example, and a demanding maximal-strength framework pursue different outcomes under different assumptions. Specialist hypertrophy volume is not a minimum requirement for a previously inactive person. Before comparing set counts, establish whether they mean sets per exercise, per muscle, per session, or across the entire week. Also distinguish easy practice from challenging work.

Effort matters, but reaching failure and producing adaptation are not synonymous. The source discussions often favor leaving a small reserve during established resistance training, while emphasizing that novices can misjudge that reserve. Power practice has another objective: preserving fast, controlled output rather than accumulating fatigue. Review actual performance, lingering fatigue, symptoms, and willingness to continue alongside attendance. Change the variable relevant to the goal; adding weight, sets, speed, and shorter rests together obscures both the response and its cause.

**Source basis:** [D335](SOURCES.md#d335), E335-02 [00:18:10], E335-06 [00:43:10], E335-07 [00:45:25], and E335-10 [00:55:47]; [D349](SOURCES.md#d349), E349-29 [01:33:31] and E349-30 [01:36:36]; [D250](SOURCES.md#d250), E250-07 [00:51:45] and E250-12 [01:19:29]; [D307](SOURCES.md#d307), E307-07 [00:21:45]; [D365](SOURCES.md#d365), E365-04 [00:18:54].

**Check:** One program reports ten sets for the whole body per session; another reports ten sets per muscle per week. What must be clarified before deciding which is a larger or more suitable dose?

**Expected answer:** Identify muscles and exercises covered, session frequency, effort, training stage, recovery, and goal. The counts use different denominators. Suitability cannot be inferred from the larger-looking number or from an expert's preferred program alone.

## HOS-DEP-EX-03 — Keep the measurement separate from its interpretation

**Purpose:** Read aerobic and wearable data without turning a convenient number into the training objective.

An aerobic result needs a protocol. Oxygen uptake, power, speed, and blood lactate describe related but different things. Faster cycling at the same measured power can reflect position or equipment rather than lower oxygen cost. A lactate concentration reflects production, transport, and disposal; the same concentration need not represent the same demand in different people. Food, hydration, preceding exercise, and test stages affect comparisons. A named training zone also depends on the system defining it.

Wearables introduce further layers: a sensor detects a signal, software calculates a statistic, and an algorithm interprets it. HRV in milliseconds is not a proprietary readiness score. Different HRV calculations, overnight averages, and short morning samples are not automatically comparable. Consistent timing and conditions make a personal trend more interpretable, but a higher value is not invariably better: recovery patterns and artifacts can complicate that inference.

Use these observations alongside sleep, symptoms, perceived effort, and actual performance. The source conversation praising one training app relied on the host's personal measurement series and explicitly acknowledged variable accuracy between users. It does not validate all users' daily targets. Elite racing examples can explain measurement limitations, but do not establish everyday health targets. Ask which decision the observation could improve, and whether a simpler, repeatable observation would answer the same question.

**Source basis:** [D294](SOURCES.md#d294), E294-03 [00:21:07], E294-10 [00:44:12], and E294-32 [02:21:56]; [D201](SOURCES.md#d201), E201-06 [00:29:14], E201-10 [00:47:46], and E201-17 [01:24:35]; [D305](SOURCES.md#d305), E305-04 [00:24:28], E305-11 [00:50:05], E305-13 [00:56:55], E305-17 [01:07:14], and E305-26 [01:32:28].

**Check:** A learner's overnight HRV is 55 milliseconds, while a different app gives a morning readiness score of 80. Can the learner conclude that recovery improved by 25 points?

**Expected answer:** No. The values differ in scale, calculation, sampling period, and interpretation. Compare like measurements within a consistent protocol, then relate the trend to context and performance. Neither number alone establishes fitness or readiness for a particular session.

## HOS-DEP-EX-04 — Keep a trial's conditions attached to its result

**Purpose:** Distinguish bone measurements, functional improvements, and evidence that a program applies to another person.

Bone strength and the chance of falling contribute different information about fracture vulnerability. A program might improve strength and balance, alter a bone measure, or do both. Those outcomes should remain separate. Conventional DXA reports projected bone density, while analyses estimating geometry ask another question. Hip and spine assessments also differ from the whole-body value supplied with a body-composition scan.

The bone-loading trial discussed in the sources enrolled selected postmenopausal women with low bone mass. Its intervention included supervision, conservative technique instruction, and gradual loading; several comorbidities were excluded. These features are part of the intervention that produced the result. Removing them while retaining only the heavy loads changes what is being proposed.

The reported improvements in spine density, estimated hip geometry, and functional tests are encouraging for those measured outcomes. They do not establish an equal percentage reduction in fractures, nor demonstrate that every person can reproduce the program. Measurement timing matters too: newly formed tissue must mineralize before DXA can reveal the corresponding change, making an early unchanged scan hard to interpret.

When reading a trial, retain the population, comparator, duration, supervision, progression, and measured outcomes. Then identify the missing information relevant to transfer. This creates an informed discussion about appropriate assessment and support, rather than a self-directed copy of the study's exercise schedule.

**Source basis:** [D322](SOURCES.md#d322), E322-07 [00:24:46], E322-18 [01:04:11], E322-19 [01:05:12], E322-21 [01:12:21], E322-22 [01:14:53], E322-23 [01:13:21], E322-24 [01:21:34], E322-25 [01:22:34], and E322-27 [01:25:35].

**Check:** A supervised study improves bone density and balance. Does this prove that doing its exercises alone will reduce an individual's fracture risk by the same percentage as the density improvement?

**Expected answer:** No. Density, balance, and fractures are different outcomes. The source does not establish that numerical conversion, and unsupervised exercise changes a central trial condition. Participant selection, clinical context, and progression also limit transfer.
