# Preventive lenses

This page states HealthOS's default point of view and the two creator lenses that helped shape it. Peter Attia and Bryan Johnson are named for attribution only. This independent project is not affiliated with or endorsed by either person or their companies, and it does not reproduce their courses or protocols.

## The HealthOS position

HealthOS is opinionated about prevention:

- Act before avoidable chronic disease or functional loss becomes advanced, while responding to present symptoms first.
- Define success through the person's desired life, function, cognition, emotional health, and tolerance for burden. Biomarkers are tools, not the purpose of health.
- Give cardiovascular and metabolic risk, cancer prevention, brain health, physical reserve, bone and falls, emotional health, and preventable injury a deliberate review rather than waiting for them to appear in a problem list.
- Start with high-value fundamentals: no tobacco exposure, appropriate movement and training, adequate sleep, a sustainable nutritious diet, blood-pressure and atherogenic-risk control, metabolic health, vaccination, and evidence-based screening.
- Use medication when a real indication, expected benefit, safety plan, and qualified decision owner justify it. Do not treat lifestyle and clinical care as rival camps.
- Test when the result can change a decision. Repeating a simple, comparable measure is often more useful than ordering an exotic one once.
- Preserve exact evidence, run measured action loops, and change course when outcomes or harms disagree with the plan.
- Treat relationships, purpose, access, cost, and adherence as health variables. A protocol that consumes the life it is meant to protect has failed.

The default priority order is: urgent safety, established disease and treatment gaps, large modifiable risks, missing evidence likely to change a decision, durable behavior and function, then experimental optimization. This order can change for a person's circumstances and values.

## Claim labels

Every source-specific statement should carry one of these labels:

- **Clinical guideline:** current recommendation for a defined population and jurisdiction.
- **Creator framework:** a way of organizing decisions.
- **Creator practice:** what a named expert reports using with patients or in practice.
- **Personal protocol or N=1:** what one person reports doing or observing in themselves.
- **Study or guest claim:** evidence that retains its underlying study, population, and limitations.
- **Commercial/editorial claim:** content connected to a product, service, audience, or sales context.
- **Hypothesis or experiment:** plausible or exploratory, without established personal benefit.

Do not let a familiar name upgrade the evidence class.

## Peter Attia lens

The public Attia material contributes these durable ideas:

- combine lifespan with physical, cognitive, and emotional healthspan;
- intervene earlier against chronic risk instead of relying only on late disease treatment;
- personalize decisions around absolute risk, time horizon, uncertainty, benefit, harm, and the cost of being wrong;
- take cumulative exposure seriously, especially for atherogenic particles and blood pressure;
- treat cardiorespiratory fitness, strength, stability, and future physical tasks as major preventive assets;
- use nutrition to support energy balance, metabolic health, muscle, and adherence rather than demand one named diet; and
- identify whether a number is evidence, a clinical threshold, an expert-practice target, a personal example, or a thought experiment.

HealthOS therefore tends to ask earlier questions, track ApoB and blood pressure in context, preserve muscle and aerobic reserve, and work backward from desired later-life functions. The few public Attia practice numbers included in [`CHEATSHEETS.md`](CHEATSHEETS.md) remain attributed preferences. They do not become treatment instructions.

Attia's paid curriculum, detailed target tables, private show notes, and branded assessment material are not bundled here. If a user lawfully supplies them for private use, preserve their access boundary and keep exact claims linked to the local source.

## Bryan Johnson lens

The public Bryan Johnson material contributes a different strength:

- make health behavior repeatable through environment, routine, preparation, and clear rules;
- protect sleep and reduce decisions that predictably sabotage it;
- measure inputs and outcomes consistently enough to learn from them;
- begin with food, movement, sleep, relationships, and other fundamentals before advanced therapies; and
- publish changes and stop interventions when personal data look unfavorable.

HealthOS borrows the execution discipline and feedback loop, not Johnson's identity or personal protocol. His biomarker percentiles, biological-age claims, calorie and macro intake, prescriptions, supplements, branded products, dense testing schedule, and advanced therapies describe a changing personal and commercial system. They are not general targets and must not be imported by default.

A personal result can generate a hypothesis. It cannot establish that an intervention improves lifespan, applies to another person, or beats current clinical guidance.

## How the lenses fit together

Use the Attia lens to frame long-horizon risk, function, and decision tradeoffs. Use the Johnson lens to make chosen fundamentals easier to execute and measure. Use HealthOS to preserve evidence, uncertainty, source class, learner state, and action state. Use current local clinical guidance and qualified care for diagnosis, screening, treatment, contraindications, and urgency.

When the lenses disagree:

| Question | HealthOS resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Adaptable diet or fixed menu? | Choose the pattern that safely meets the person's goal, nutritional needs, culture, outcomes, and adherence. A creator's menu is an example. |
| Dense testing or selective testing? | Require a decision question, result pathways, expected value, burden, and follow-up plan. More data is useful only when it improves a decision. |
| Biomarker optimization or lived outcome? | Use biomarkers as intermediate evidence. Function, symptoms, adverse effects, and meaningful life outcomes remain visible. |
| Advanced therapy or fundamentals? | Close urgent and high-value gaps first. Experimental interventions need a clear evidence label, qualified oversight, monitoring, and a stop rule. |
| Personal biological-age result or clinical risk? | Keep biological-age measures in a research or wellness layer unless validated guidance gives them a clinical role. Never translate a clock change into years of life gained. |
| Rigid protocol or flexible plan? | Preserve the parts whose consistency produces value and adapt the parts that create harm, exclusion, or unsustainable burden. |

## Choosing a metric or target

For every optimization target, record:

1. the outcome it is meant to improve;
2. the exact metric and repeatable measurement protocol;
3. the number's role and claim label;
4. the source, date, population, and jurisdiction;
5. the person's baseline and relevant risks;
6. who owns the decision;
7. the intervention, burden, safety limits, and review point; and
8. what result would lead to continuing, adapting, or stopping.

If those fields cannot be filled, the target is not ready to guide action.
